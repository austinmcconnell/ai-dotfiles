# Audit Output Template

Structure for the written stress-test. Adapt depth to the piece, but keep the section order — it
forces steelman-before-critique and separates the three quality axes at the end.

## Structure

```markdown
## [One-line framing]

[If relevant: the single most important caveat up front — e.g. the piece is dated in a way that
makes its factual anchors unverifiable, or its thesis rests on one source. State it before analysis.]

## The verifiability frame

What I can and cannot verify:

- **Cannot verify:** [time-boxed / future-dated / private facts — flagged load-bearing-but-unconfirmed]
- **Can/did verify:** [checkable facts — and what verification found]
- **Assessed on internal merits:** [the reasoning, forecasts, rhetoric]

## The core structure / thesis

[The one sentence the piece exists to establish, and the main claims supporting it. Naming the
rhetorical architecture here is often the most illuminating move.]

## Where the argument is strongest

[Steelman FIRST. What does the piece get right? Which mechanisms are sound, which claims are
well-supported? A critique with no "what's right" section is incomplete and less credible.]

## Where it breaks down

[Grouped by failure type, each citing specific text and a specific framework check:]

1. **[Failure type — e.g. single-cause attribution]** — [quote/paraphrase the specific passage],
   [why it fails, which check: counterfactual not established / falsifiability / etc.]
1. **[Failure type]** — ...

## The verification problem (if applicable)

[If load-bearing facts couldn't be verified, say so explicitly and list them. An unverifiable
linchpin is a finding, not an omission. State that if these premises are false, the conclusion fails
regardless of the reasoning quality.]

## Bottom line

Separate the three axes explicitly — a piece can score very differently on each:

- **As rhetoric:** [how well-constructed / persuasive]
- **As analysis:** [does the reasoning hold; what are the fatal tells]
- **As prediction:** [is it falsifiable; is the confidence earned]

[Close with the honest reading: what the piece establishes vs. what it asserts, and how much a
reader should update on it.]
```

## Notes

- **Do not force all sections** if the piece is short or the audit is quick — but always keep the
  verifiability frame (when facts are in play), the steelman, and the three-axis bottom line.
- **Quote or closely paraphrase** the specific text for each failure. An audit that gestures at
  "bias" without citing where is not an audit.
- **Size the critique to the evidence.** If the piece is sound, the "where it breaks down" section
  may be short or empty — say so rather than manufacturing weaknesses. The same discipline the skill
  applies to the article applies to the audit.
