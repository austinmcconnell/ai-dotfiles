# Worked Example

An end-to-end application of the workflow to a real article, to calibrate how much depth an audit
warrants and how the framework checks turn into findings. The piece: Dean Blundell, "The Smartest
Money on Earth Just Quit Trump's America" (Substack, dated Sept 8 2026,
<https://deanblundell.substack.com/p/the-smartest-money-on-earth-just>), predicting a US economic
collapse, built on a "smartest money is fleeing" gold narrative and a month-by-month forecast
through 2028.

This example is illustrative, not a script — the *moves* transfer, the specific findings do not.

Why the facts were unverifiable (this matters for the example's own honesty): the article's factual
anchors describe events dated at or after the time of the audit, which the auditing tools could not
independently confirm against reporting. "Unverifiable" here means exactly that — *not* confirmed
and *not* refuted — not "fabricated." Naming that state precisely is the whole point of step 2; the
article being real is what makes it a fair demonstration rather than a straw target.

## Step 1–2: Read fully, then state the verifiability frame

The essay was dated in a way that made its factual anchors (a 2026 US–Iran war, central-bank gold
repatriation figures, specific Treasury yields, a named Fed Chair, a Supreme Court tariff ruling)
**unverifiable** from available tools. The audit led with this: the reasoning and rhetoric can be
assessed on their merits, but the factual premises cannot be confirmed true or false — so the
conclusion is weaker than the confident prose implies *regardless* of which way the facts fall.

Stating this up front is the honesty move from step 2. It prevents laundering unverified premises
into a verdict.

## Step 3–4: Decompose, tag, run the checks

The essay's architecture, once decomposed, was the source of its problems:

1. A vivid **undated human anchor** ("the woman in Dayton"), explicitly labeled "a projection, not a
   prophecy."
1. A wall of **specific numbers** (14% of gold, 4.8% yields, $118 oil) that read as established
   fact.
1. A **month-by-month forecast** in flat declarative tense.
1. An **appeal to one op-ed columnist** for the load-bearing "it's permanent" claim.

The checks turned that structure into findings:

- **Epistemic-status tagging (check 1):** steps 1 and 3 are the same category — speculation — but
  only step 1 is labeled as such. The forecasts are written in the *register* of reporting. Central
  tell. (ICD 203 std 3: fact vs. judgment must be distinguished.)
- **Causal attribution (check 2):** yields moving 3.95% → 4.8% was attributed *entirely* to policy.
  Yields respond to many inputs; no counterfactual established. Correlation on a timeline. (SEP
  counterfactual; Bradford Hill temporality is necessary but not sufficient.)
- **Precision vs. methodology (check 3):** "$4.50–5 gas," "15–20% correction," "unemployment 6–7%"
  by named months — point-and-range forecasts with no model or probability. False precision.
  (Tetlock overconfidence; ICD 203 std 2 probability lexicon.)
- **Falsifiability (check 4):** "projection, not a prophecy — stay on this path and this is where it
  ends" is the ad hoc rescue: any miss is attributed to "the path changed." Popper's SEP §9 is
  directly on point — unconditional prophecy is impossible for non-isolated systems like "modern
  society," so a dated month-by-month macro forecast is epistemically overreaching by construction.
- **Source load & one-sidedness (check 5):** the emotional climax ("it survives your ballot,"
  elections can't fix it) rested almost entirely on *one* named op-ed. Single point of failure. And
  the ledger was one-directional — no serious counter-scenario (dollar resilience, market depth,
  prior-crisis absorption) was engaged, only listed to be dismissed. (ICD 203 std 4/6; weak-man.)
- **Tone as a tell (check 6):** "fucking morons," "the regime," "the arsonist" — persuasion by
  contempt; each loaded term marks where an argument should be. (Heuristic, not a sourced test — it
  pointed to *where* the missing arguments were, confirmed by the other checks.)

## Step 5: Verify decision-critical facts

Here the facts were time-boxed and unverifiable, so verification meant *confirming they were
unverifiable* and naming them as load-bearing — itself the finding. In a normal audit this step is
where you fetch the 2–3 authoritative sources the thesis rests on and confirm or refute them.

## Step 6: The bottom line (three axes)

- **As rhetoric:** well-constructed, emotionally powerful.
- **As analysis:** three fatal tells — single-cause attribution presented as proof, false-precision
  forecasting presented as reporting, a load-bearing "it's permanent" thesis resting on one op-ed.
- **As prediction:** structured to be unfalsifiable.

Honest reading: a real set of economic risks (the stagflation-trap, term-premium, and pro-cyclical
safety-net *mechanisms* are legitimate), dramatized well past what the cited evidence supports,
wrapped around factual claims that couldn't be verified. The piece consistently converted "plausible
and directionally concerning" into "certain and precisely dated" — and that conversion was not
earned.

## The transferable lesson

The mechanisms in a piece can be sound while its *attributions, precision, and certainty* are
overstated. The audit's job is to find exactly where "plausible" was silently upgraded to "certain,"
name the upgrade, and size the reader's trust to what was actually established.
